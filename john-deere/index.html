The problem

Engineering knowledge is scattered across too many disconnected systems

Design intent, risk assessments, technical specs, hydraulic and electrical schematics, requirements, code, tickets, and tribal knowledge all live in separate places — with no reliable layer keeping them connected as the team develops.

Where engineering knowledge lives today

Three compounding issues

Why fragmentation keeps costing the team

01

Weak workflow durability

Because knowledge is fragmented across software and hardware teams, workflows break down and context is lost. There is no reusable layer that keeps product knowledge continuous.

02

No cross-system context

A change can ripple system-wide, but relationships across schematics, controllers, and platforms can't be seen or reasoned about in one place.

03

Unstructured, siloed knowledge

Critical information lives in Excel files on shared drives, in individual engineers' heads, and in documents that can't be structured — with no way to bring it together.

Why today's stack falls short

The current AI stack doesn't solve the core problem

The team relies on GitHub Copilot as its predominant AI tool, with access to other foundation models — but everyone is "navigating their own waters," which is neither cohesive nor enterprise-safe.

GitHub Copilot

Lacks the connectors and enterprise context

With only a handful of connections, it can't reach across John Deere's systems — so answers come back generic, without the context needed to fold into engineering work.

Claude & standalone models

Not secure across the enterprise

Standalone models can't safely span John Deere's internal systems. Each team running its own tools introduces security risk and gives no shared, enterprise-wide context.

What the team needs

Requirements for a context & connectivity layer beneath your tools

Not a replacement for Copilot or GitHub — a durable foundation that sits underneath them and makes every tool smarter.

1

Unify structured & unstructured knowledge

Bring code, tickets, docs, and multimodal content — PDF schematics, diagrams, technical specs — into a single, permissions-aware layer.

2

Preserve native security permissions

Users only ever see what they already have access to, with no additional security work to bring permissions over.

3

A durable, reusable retrieval foundation

So internal agents and workflows don't rediscover the same context every time they run.

4

Enable an agentic development workflow

Across software, electrical, and hardware domains — connecting into where the team already works, like Copilot and GitHub.
