Executive Summary

A stabilizing quarter carried by premium mix and share gains.

Goodyear reported Q2 net sales of $4.25B (-4.8% YoY; just -1.4% organic after the Chemical and Dunlop divestitures), ahead of consensus, with an adjusted loss of $(0.61) per share and total segment operating income of $36M. Tire volume of 36.5M units fell just 4.0% — a marked improvement from the 12% decline in Q1 as destocking moderated. Premium ≥18-inch tires reached 43% of the consumer portfolio and OE volume and share grew in every region. Analysts pressed on the path back to profitability, the Fayetteville footprint action, and the inflation and Middle East raw-material outlook. Tone was cautiously constructive; the volume-moderation and premium-mix narrative was the most-discussed theme of the call.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • 1Sequential stabilization. Volume fell 4.0% versus a 12% decline in Q1; management sees Q3 volumes roughly flat year over year.
  • 2Premium mix is the engine. ≥18-inch tires now 43% of the consumer portfolio, with 1,600+ new Goodyear and Cooper SKUs launching in 2026.
  • 3OE share gains in every region; EMEA marked its tenth consecutive quarter of consumer OE share gains.
  • 4Goodyear Forward is delivering — $95M in-quarter benefit and ~$325M guided for FY2026; the Fayetteville closure targets ~$270M of annual segment-income improvement from 2028.
  • 5Balance sheet strengthening — free cash flow improved $318M YoY to $(69)M and net debt fell $722M YoY to $6.3B; Asia Pacific led operations at $63M SOI / 12.7% margin.

Scott DeakinInterim EVP & Chief Financial Officer · Financial review[00:10]

Thank you, Mark. Second-quarter net sales of $4.25 billion decreased 4.8%, or just 1.4% organically after the Chemical and Dunlop divestitures. Total segment operating income was $36 million, a 0.8% margin.

Lower volume was a $132 million headwind, tariffs and other costs $100 million, and inflation $53 million — partially offset by $123 million of favorable price/mix versus raw materials and $95 million of Goodyear Forward benefits. Free cash flow improved $318 million year over year to negative $69 million, and we reduced net debt by $722 million to $6.3 billion.

Scott DeakinInterim EVP & Chief Financial Officer · Regional review[00:16]

By region, the Americas posted a $10 million segment operating loss as replacement volume fell 13% — better than the 23% decline in Q1 — while EMEA improved its loss to $17 million and marked its tenth consecutive quarter of consumer OE share gains. Asia Pacific was the standout, with $63 million of segment operating income at a 12.7% margin, driven by premium ≥18-inch growth and China OE.

Scott DeakinInterim EVP & Chief Financial Officer · Guidance[00:22]

For the third quarter, we expect global unit volumes roughly flat year over year, improving from the first half. We anticipate about $110 million of price/mix benefit and roughly $70 million from Goodyear Forward, with full-year Goodyear Forward benefits of approximately $325 million.

Offsetting these, about $95 million of higher inflation and other costs — including roughly $60 million of core inflation — a $20 million raw-material headwind as Middle East commodity costs flow through on our typical four-to-six-month lag, and about $10 million of tariffs.
