Executive Summary

A beat-and-raise quarter carried by AI-driven efficiency.

Motorola Solutions reported Q2 revenue of $2.85B (+9% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $3.42, both ahead of consensus, and raised full-year guidance. Management directly addressed component and memory cost pressure and framed its AI-native operating model as the structural offset. Analysts pressed on margin durability, the pace of M&A integration, and the credibility of the 2035 transformation. Tone was confident; the AI narrative was the most-discussed theme of the call.

Chief Executive OfficerOpening remarks[00:02]

Thank you, and good afternoon, everyone. This was a quarter of disciplined execution against a demanding backdrop. Revenue grew 9% to $2.85 billion, operating margin expanded 130 basis points, and we closed the quarter with a record $14.3 billion in backlog — the clearest signal we have of the durability of demand.

Two themes define the quarter. First, our mission-critical franchise remains resilient even as the industry navigates real component and memory cost pressure. Second — and this is where we are pulling ahead — we are becoming an AI-native enterprise, in operating reality, not slogan. We are not adding AI to our business; we are rebuilding how our business runs around it.

JasonChief Financial Officer · Financial review[00:08]

Thank you. Second-quarter revenue of $2.85 billion was up 9% year over year, with strength across both Products & Systems Integration and Software & Services. Non-GAAP earnings per share came in at $3.42, up 12%, and we generated $610 million of free cash flow.

Operating margin of 28.4% expanded 130 basis points. Let me be direct about what the market is watching: component and memory costs remain elevated. We have built a foolproof glide path over the coming quarters — disciplined sourcing, supplier diversification, and structural efficiency from our AI-native operating model that is offsetting a meaningful share of that inflation.

Chiara PandhiBusiness AI & Strategy[00:16]

Thank you, Jason. I want to connect the margin story to what is happening operationally, because they are the same story.

Over the past year we have taken the enterprise AI-native across operations, procurement, quality, portfolio management, and M&A. We now have 27 AI-native workflows in production, up from 18 a quarter ago, coordinated through a single, governed intelligence layer rather than assembled workflow by workflow.

This matters for two reasons. First, integration velocity — each new business becomes searchable, connected knowledge on arrival, with every access control preserved. Second, structural efficiency — the same layer lets a procurement or operations leader ask one question and get a trusted, cited answer across every system, which is precisely how we are absorbing component-cost pressure without diluting margin. Our goal for 2035 is a lean, AI-native enterprise where every decision is grounded in the whole of what we know.

JasonChief Financial Officer · Guidance[00:23]

Reflecting first-half performance and the strength of our backlog, we are raising full-year guidance. We now expect full-year 2026 revenue of approximately $11.6 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $14.05 to $14.20. For the third quarter, we anticipate revenue of roughly $2.98 billion, up 7 to 8% year over year.
